Forensic engineering FAQs.

What does a forensic engineer investigate?
Forensic engineers investigate the cause, extent and contributing factors of property damage, construction defects, and structural or component failures — developing findings supported by physical evidence, documentation, codes and engineering principles.
When should a forensic engineer be retained?
Early enough to evaluate relevant physical evidence before conditions change, repairs occur, or potentially important evidence is altered or lost.
What types of property damage does TCE investigate?
Hurricane, wind, hail, water intrusion, fire and lightning losses, plus construction defects — for all building components from structure to envelope to interior finishes.
Does TCE provide expert witness and litigation support services?
Yes. Reports are written to be defensible under oath, and the engineer who inspected the matter stands behind the opinions through deposition and testimony when required.
What information should I have available before contacting a forensic engineer?
Whatever exists: photographs, plans, repair records, prior reports or testing. An engineer can tell you what is technically relevant and whether further investigation is justified.
Where does TCE provide forensic engineering services?
Nationwide, with licensure in 30 states, D.C. and Puerto Rico — and current priority markets in Texas, Florida, Illinois, Ohio and Arkansas.
