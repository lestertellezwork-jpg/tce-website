Water intrusion:
finding the real source.

Water never shows up where it enters. How engineers trace a leak to its true source — and why that answer decides the repair and the claim.

By Eric Trillas, P.E. · Trillas Consulting Engineers

Of every loss type we investigate, water intrusion is the one most often misdiagnosed — because water almost never appears where it enters. A stain on a fourth-floor ceiling can originate at a roof penetration forty feet away; efflorescence on a slab can be ground water, a slab leak, or a vapor-drive problem; a “window leak” is frequently a wall assembly leak that only shows at the window. Finding the true source is an engineering exercise, and the source determines everything: the repair, the cost, and — on an insurance claim — whether the loss is covered at all.

Where Water Actually Enters

  • Roof systems — punctures, failed flashings, displaced tiles or membrane seams (often storm-initiated, see our tile roof guide)
  • Building envelope — stucco cracks, failed sealant joints, wall penetrations, parapets
  • Windows & doors — failed glazing seals, missing pan flashings, installation deficiencies
  • Plumbing — supply, drain, and HVAC condensate failures inside walls and above ceilings
  • Below-slab and ground water — hydrostatic pressure, vapor drive through slabs, failed waterproofing

How an Engineering Investigation Traces It

A TCE water intrusion investigation works from evidence, not assumption:

  • Mapping moisture with meters and infrared thermography to define the wet footprint — which rarely matches the visible stain
  • Tracing the pathway from exit point back to entry point through the assembly
  • Water testing where warranted — controlled spray or flood testing that reproduces the leak and proves the source
  • Reviewing construction documents: where the waterproofing, flashings, and slopes were supposed to be, versus what was built
  • Distinguishing storm-created openings from long-term deficiencies and maintenance conditions — the distinction most claims turn on

Why the Source Determines the Claim

Insurance outcomes on water losses hinge on causation: sudden-and-accidental versus long-term seepage, storm-created opening versus pre-existing deficiency, construction defect versus maintenance. A documented engineering opinion on the entry point and mechanism is the difference between an argument and an answer. Our findings distinguish what the evidence supports, what it does not, and what cannot be determined — written to be defensible under oath.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a water damage assessment?

An engineering evaluation that documents where water is entering, the pathway it travels, the damage it has caused, and the conditions that contributed — producing findings an insurer, board, or court can rely on.

Why do water leaks get misdiagnosed?

Water travels. It enters at one location, runs along framing, pipes, or waterproofing layers, and appears somewhere else entirely. Patching where the stain shows treats the symptom and guarantees a repeat.

When should an engineer investigate instead of a contractor?

When the cause is disputed, the damage is recurring after repairs, a claim or litigation is involved, or multiple possible sources exist — roof, envelope, windows, plumbing, or ground water — and the answer carries financial consequences.
